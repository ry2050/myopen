How to play

How to play Taiwanese mahjong

Taiwanese mahjong is a four-player game played with 16 tiles in hand. You draw and discard until your tiles form five sets and a pair. It is not the tile-matching solitaire game that shares its name: this is the table game, played with three other people.

The tiles

144 tiles

108Three suits: characters, dots and bamboo, 1 to 9, four of each
28Honors: east, south, west, north winds and red, green, white dragons, four of each
8Flowers: four seasons and four flowers, one of each

Flowers never go into your hand. When you draw one, you put it face up on the table and draw a replacement. A flower whose number matches your seat (East 1, South 2, West 3, North 4) adds tai: see flowers in the scoring list.

A hand, step by step

How a hand is played

  1. The deal: everyone gets 16 tiles. The dealer takes one more and starts by discarding. Any flowers are set aside and replaced.
  2. Draw and discard: turns go counterclockwise. Draw one tile, discard one tile, so you always hold 16.
  3. Chow: take the tile the player before you just discarded to finish a run of three in one suit, like 3-4-5 dots.
  4. Pung and kong: take anyone's discard to make three of a kind (pung) or four of a kind (kong). After a kong you draw a replacement tile.
  5. Win: when your tiles form five sets and a pair. You can win on someone's discard or on a tile you drew yourself (self-draw).
  6. Draw game: if the wall runs out and nobody has won, the hand ends and the dealer keeps the deal.

Winning

A winning hand: five sets and a pair

Your score for the hand is the sum of every scoring pattern it contains, counted in tai. The scoring list has every pattern with its value.

The dealer

Dealer streaks

If the dealer wins, or the hand is a draw, the same player deals again, and each repeat adds tai to hands involving the dealer. When anyone else wins, the deal moves to the next player. Once every player has dealt, a wind round is over; the table agrees beforehand how many rounds to play.

In Taiwan Mahjong Now, you set the number of hands for single-player in Settings, and the table host sets the rounds for online play.

House rules

Rules in Taiwan Mahjong Now

Every table plays Taiwanese mahjong a little differently. This is how the app does it; the rules are fixed:

  • Two players call a win on the same tile: only one wins, the one closest to the discarder in turn order.
  • Priority: win beats pung and kong, which beat chow. Everyone who can call gets to answer before the decision is made, so speed doesn't matter.
  • Passing on a win: you can still win later. Only the first-discard ready hands drop back to the normal 1 tai.
  • Declaring ready: adds 1 tai and locks your hand. You can't chow, pung or kong after that, and drawing and discarding are automatic, but you can still win or pass.
  • Not included: multiple winners on one discard, the bao rule (holding one player responsible for a big hand), seven pairs, and a tai cap.
